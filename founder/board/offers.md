# Offers lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

**Market check:** the pain is real (finding, managing and checking a contractor, plus homes that need repainting after the monsoon). The buyers are easy to find: Turbolux already has Kochi dealers and blog content on the exact questions these buyers search for (bubbling, how much paint a 2BHK needs, when to paint in Kerala). Two things are unknown: whether enough buyers will pay over ₹70,000, and whether the market is growing. **Value equation:** dream outcome 7/10, likelihood 4/10, time 6/10, effort 8/10. It fails where most offers fail: on likelihood, because there is no proof yet.

## What is strong
- **Effort is close to zero for the buyer.** One order, one price, one brand, no contractor to find or supervise. This is the strongest part of the offer.
- **One party owns the result.** Today, when paint bubbles, the painter blames the paint and the paint shop blames the painter. Here the paint maker stands behind both, so the guarantee is believable in a way a local contractor's is not.
- **The urgency is real.** The season runs Sept to Jan and trained crews are limited. "Limited slots this season" is true, so it can be used honestly.
- **It does not compete on price.** It is pitched as premium and all-inclusive, not as cheaper than a dealer plus a contractor.
- **Distribution already exists.** Dealers, the website and SEO content mean the first buyers do not have to be paid for from zero.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is no proof, and the crews do not exist yet.** The whole promise (fixed date, no bubbling, trained crew) rests on crews that have not been hired, trained or tested. A few late or badly done jobs in the pilot season would hurt the core paint brand and the dealers, not just this service line. The guarantee then turns into a cost. How many crews can be trained, and what each job really costs, are open questions (/founder-cfo).
2. **"A 2BHK" is not a fixed unit, so the fixed price may not hold.** Floor area, wall condition after the monsoon (damp, cracks, fungus) and exterior access vary a lot, and a flat owner may not control the exterior at all. Two things can go wrong. If the price holds, the margin shrinks on the bigger or worse jobs. If the site visit changes the price, "fixed price" stops being true and buyers stop trusting it. Paint cost per job and painter wages are unknown (/founder-cfo).
3. **Buyers may see it as a markup, and the trade may push back.** If buyers compare ₹78,000 with "dealer paint plus my local painter" and see the same thing for more money, the offer becomes a commodity. Competitors' prices are unknown (/founder-competitors) and so is willingness to pay (/founder-consumer). Separately, painters and contractors who buy Turbolux paint through dealers today may stop recommending it if the brand now competes with them for jobs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the buyer: someone in a flat or in an independent house? In a flat, who decides about the exterior? (/founder-consumer)
- Would a Kochi buyer pay ₹78,000, and which segment says yes? (/founder-consumer) What do the alternatives cost? (/founder-competitors)
- What does the guarantee actually say: how long it lasts, which defects it covers, what the remedy is, and how fast someone responds? What does it cost to back? (/founder-cfo)
- How many crews can be trained before September? Will they be employees or partner contractors? Who checks the work?
- Can the site visit change the price? If so, what is the rule, written down before the buyer books?
- What proof will the first buyer see: past jobs, before/after photos, a named crew lead?
- How long does a job take, and what is asked of the household (moving furniture, staying elsewhere, cleaning up)?
- How many 2BHK repaints happen in Kochi each season? No step answers this fully. /founder-consumer gives buy rates, but the total count needs outside data.
- What would have to be in the package for the price to double and buyers still see it as the obvious choice?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Make the price rule clear.** Publish fixed price bands by size and condition, and separate interior-only (flats) from interior plus exterior (houses). After that, the site visit can only confirm the band, never raise it. /founder-cfo shows a positive margin in each band once real paint cost and wages replace the estimates.
2. **Prove it before scaling.** Run a small first batch of pilot jobs and record whether each one finished on its date, before/after photos and buyer reviews. Open wider booking only after that batch delivers.
3. **Write a guarantee that can be paid out.** Put the workmanship guarantee in plain terms and have /founder-cfo cost and reserve for it. If the numbers allow, add a finish-date guarantee.
4. **Build the stack from the buyer's problems**, and keep the parts that are cheap to deliver: colour help, paint quantity sealed and checked on site, damp and fungus treatment, furniture and floor covering, clean-up, and a touch-up check after the next monsoon. Each part is costed by /founder-cfo.
5. **Make the trade part of it.** Pay dealers a referral fee, and hire and train existing painters into the crews rather than competing with them.
