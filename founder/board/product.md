# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **It fits in one sentence:** "Turbolux repaints your 2BHK, paint and painters, for one fixed price, by a fixed date, with a guarantee." At this stage that clarity is rare.
- **It starts from the experience, not the product.** The buyer's pain is not the paint. It is finding, managing and checking a contractor. The package takes that job away from the homeowner; it does not add features.
- **It closes the biggest seam in today's journey.** Today the paint comes from a dealer, the labour from a separate contractor, and nobody owns the result. One brand, one order and one guarantee is the "own the whole experience" move.
- **It is already focused:** one city, one home size, one tier, one season.
- **The brand has a reason to care about the parts nobody sees.** Turbolux already publishes content on paint bubbling. The guarantee puts the brand in charge of the application, not just the tin.
- **The touchpoints already exist:** dealers, a website, and SEO content that answers this buyer's exact questions.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The crew is the product, and the crew does not exist yet.** The customer spends days with strangers in their home. That is the product they experience, not the paint. The brief lists crew numbers, training, wages and employment model as unknown. Surface preparation is the craft nobody sees, and it is the first corner a rushed crew cuts. A Sept to Jan season pushes crews to rush. If prep is skipped, the failure shows up months later as bubbling, under the Turbolux guarantee and the Turbolux name. A bad crew damages the paint brand, not only the new service line.
2. **The "fixed price" may not survive the site visit.** A "2BHK" is not one product: floor area, wall condition after the monsoon, damp, cracks, and house versus flat all vary. If the site visit often re-quotes, the core promise breaks at the first moment after booking. For a flat, the exterior may not be the owner's to paint, so "interior + exterior" may not fit part of the stated audience. This is an open question, not a known fact.
3. **Too many handoffs behind one promise.** The chain runs dealer or website, then site visit, then crew, then handover, then a guarantee claim months later. Every handoff is a seam. Dealers today sell paint to the contractors this package replaces. If their role and reward are unclear, the first touchpoint is the weakest one. If no single person owns the customer from booking to claim, "one order" is only true on the website. The fixed finish date also has to hold through weather in season.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Is the sentence above the product? If not, what is the one sentence?
2. Walk me through the morning the crew arrives and the hour the job is handed over. What does the homeowner see, sign and keep?
3. When the site visit finds damp or cracks, what happens to the ₹78,000? How will that feel to someone who booked online?
4. For flats, whose is the exterior? If it is not the owner's, what is the flat product?
5. Who does the homeowner call when the crew does not show on day 3, or when a wall bubbles in month 8?
6. Are crews Turbolux employees or contractors wearing Turbolux shirts? Who checks the prep before paint goes on?
7. What would you cut: exterior, flats, or everything but one finish?
8. Why would a dealer sell a package that replaces their own contractor customers?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pilot the in-home experience before selling at volume.** Run a small number of real Kochi jobs end to end; /founder-cfo sets the number. Each job needs a written prep and handover standard, a Turbolux check before any paint goes on, and photos of the prep kept on file.
2. **Make the fixed price true.** Define in writing what a "2BHK" package covers and what counts as an extra, and show it before the site visit. Track how often the visit changes the price. If it changes often, narrow the product (for example houses only, or interior only) until the price holds.
3. **One owner of each customer from booking to guarantee claim,** plus a dealer role that dealers actually want.
4. **The numbers hold.** /founder-cfo shows the package earns money at ₹78,000 after crew, paint and guarantee claims. Trained crew capacity must cover the Sept to Jan bookings before sales open beyond it.
