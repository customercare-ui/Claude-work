# Monopoly lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market with clear edges.** One city (Kochi), one home type (2BHK), one season (Sept to Jan). That is the shape the Monopoly lens looks for: a market narrow enough to take a big share of before expanding.
- **Distribution already exists.** Most new service businesses have a product and no way to reach buyers. This one starts with Kochi dealers and a website whose blog already answers the questions a repainting homeowner asks (bubbling, how much paint a 2BHK needs, the best time to paint in Kerala). The first customers can arrive through channels Turbolux already owns.
- **Only the maker can guarantee the whole job.** A contractor cannot vouch for the paint; a paint brand selling through dealers cannot vouch for the labour. Owning both is what makes a guarantee against bubbling and peeling credible. That is the seed of a brand advantage.
- **Possible scale and know-how advantages.** Paint at the maker's own cost and crews scheduled densely in one city could give a cost edge a lone contractor cannot match, and every job teaches what survives Kerala's monsoon. Neither is proven yet.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No moat: the bundle is a business model, not a breakthrough.** Paint plus painter plus guarantee is an incremental improvement that any paint brand with dealers can copy, and other brands, national ones included, may already sell it in Kochi. If they do, this is entry into a fight, not ownership of a market. Open question: who already sells a bundled paint-and-crew repaint within reach of a Kochi homeowner, at what price and with what guarantee (/founder-competitors).
2. **Channel conflict with the paint business itself.** Today the homeowner buys paint from a dealer and hires a contractor separately. A paint brand that sells labour competes with those contractors, and if they influence which paint gets bought, they can steer homeowners to other brands. That puts the core paint business at risk, not only the pilot. Open questions: how contractors and dealers respond (the founder) and what a dealer earns on a package sale, costed into the unit economics (/founder-cfo).
3. **The crews are the asset, and they can walk.** A Turbolux-trained crew can leave, buy Turbolux paint from a dealer and do the same job cheaper, taking the know-how with them. A Sept-to-Jan season also leaves crews idle most of the year, which weakens any scale advantage. Open questions: painter wage rates, how many crews can be trained, and the cost of keeping them (/founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about Kochi homeowners and repainting that other paint brands and local contractors do not? (the secret)
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the season to start?
- On which one thing is this ten times better than a dealer plus a contractor: certainty of price, of finish date, of finish quality? How will you show it? (/founder-consumer)
- How many 2BHK repaints happen in Kochi in one season, and what share can you take in year one? (/founder-competitors for the market, /founder-consumer for demand)
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost to win? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- What does the guarantee cost when claims come in after the next monsoon? (/founder-cfo)
- What stops a competitor matching the package by month six?
- After Kochi 2BHK post-monsoon, what is the next market: bigger homes, other Kerala cities, interior-only work off-season?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. /founder-competitors shows no other brand already owns this bundle in Kochi, or names one dimension (for example the guarantee's terms or finish-date certainty) where Turbolux is clearly far better, not slightly better.
2. Before launch, a dealer and contractor plan that makes them partners rather than competitors (for example, dealers earn on each package, and good local contractors can become certified Turbolux crews).
3. A crew model that keeps trained crews loyal and busy beyond the season, with its cost carried in /founder-cfo.
4. /founder-cfo shows a margin at ₹78,000 after labour, paint at cost and a guarantee reserve, and /founder-consumer shows Kochi 2BHK owners pay for certainty rather than comparing on price alone.
5. A stated target share of the Kochi season, set once the market size is known, so "own the small market first" can be measured.
